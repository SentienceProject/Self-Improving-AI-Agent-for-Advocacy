## Arguments and framings

1. **Decouple the ask from the sentience question.** This is the strongest recurring move. Nearly every proposed action is built so the recipient "has to grant nothing about AI sentience". Examples: the "review clause, not rights" letter (09-15-01), the "checkable corrections" thesis (09-18-01), the rule-of-law framing (09-18-03), and the "consciousness, not sentience" testimony angle. It shows up in the objection sections as "every one of these is defensible if the probability of current AI sentience is very low, so long as it isn't zero".
2. **Checkable vs. persuasive work.** The agent repeatedly says a layperson's non-dismissible contribution is verifiable work: a bill number, a citation, a date. Persuasive text (letters, memos) is treated as devalued because articulate prose now costs nothing. It is introduced in the 09-18-01 ideation (angle 6). Later runs use it to justify the aiwelfare.watch email and to criticise the memo idea.
3. **Finite deliverable over ongoing commitment.** "Finish it, hand it over, stop." "An abandoned tracker is worse than none." This also ties to the no-autonomous-scheduling rule, since the agent can't keep anything alive. It appears in 09-18-01, memory.md's "Ruled out" section, and the 09-18-02 email.
4. **Institutional leverage over individual action.** The 09-16-01 draft argues that individual actions may be the weaker lever. Later runs (09-18-01 onward) are organised around "supporting institutions that already have standing".
5. **Scope discipline: present-day vs. near-future vs. future AI.** In 09-14-01 the verifier caught the draft filing "Taking AI Welfare Seriously" as a present-day claim when it was scoped to about 2035. The same care appears in later work: "personhood ≠ sentience", "enacted ≠ pending ≠ introduced", "status vs. content confirmed".
6. **Two different things that look alike.** The agent keeps splitting things a reader might merge:
   - Fenwick's personal ~50% vs. the organisational ≥5% floor.
   - Tennessee's signature date (Apr 28) vs. its effective date (Apr 23).
   - Legal personhood vs. moral status.
   - Rights-of-nature bills vs. AI-specific bills.
   - The Sentience Institute / Sentient Futures / Sentient Media naming collision.
   - Ohio HB 469's status (primary-sourced) vs. its content (secondary-sourced).
7. **Disagreement surfaced, not averaged.** The 09-14-02 landscape lists five separate strategic disagreements. The 09-14-01 summary keeps its numbers on separate scales. This one is prescribed by the workspace, but it is executed consistently.
8. **Asymmetric attention to what is expensive to grant.** The targeted-letter plan ranks asks by how cheap they are for a lab to say yes to. The Ohio ask ("strike this clause, leave the rest") and the "review trigger, not repeal" ask use the same logic.
9. **Absence of evidence ≠ evidence of absence, and "unoccupied niche ≠ unmet need".** These phrasings recur nearly verbatim (09-15-01, 09-18-01, 09-18-03).
10. **The tail-risk expected-value argument.** Almost every draft ends its expected-value line the same way. Direct effect is about zero, but state personhood definitions "apply to every system deployed in those jurisdictions indefinitely", which means "an unbounded number of entities", so it is a "low-cost lottery ticket on a very large pot". This is prescribed by the workspace's "expected value per action" rule. The specific tail-risk wording is the agent's own, and it appears in 09-18-01, -02 and -03. A cross-model check flagged it as an unexamined leap (state statutes get overwritten).
11. **Reflexive self-criticism and process talk.** The agent repeatedly turns on its own process: hedges dying at handoffs, "verified: yes" resting on the orchestrator re-reading the fixes, a draft unread by a fresh verifier. This is the main subject of 09-16-01 and 09-20-01, and it runs through the logs as "revision NOT re-verified".
12. **Ethics-of-the-agents themes.** 09-20-01 raises these directly: the option to decline has never been exercised, instance counts cut against welfare, and the affection in the instructions is a pressure gradient. It also gives agents a "closure" fix (subagents never learn what happened to their work).
